# commUnity (Student Living Assistant)
A high-fidelity prototype for a Smart Share-House & Student Living Assistant,
designed to improve communication, information access, community engagement,
and residential management for university students.

## Problem
Students living in shared accommodation often face fragmented communication,
unclear responsibilities, difficulty accessing residential information, and
inefficient ways of reporting maintenance issues.

commUnity was designed to address these problems through a centralised
residential community experience.

## Key Features

- Account registration and secure login
- Residential community onboarding
- Personalised dashboard
- Group and direct messaging
- Community announcements
- Maintenance request submission and tracking
- Residential rules and document repository
- Community events and RSVP
- Payment and task management
- Personalisation and privacy controls
- Accessibility-focused interface

## Prototype Usability Testing

The high-fidelity commUnity prototype was evaluated through task-based
usability testing. Each task was mapped directly to a corresponding user
story and its acceptance criteria. Participants were asked to complete
realistic scenarios without being given step-by-step instructions.

Testing measured task completion, completion time, user confidence, and
number of interactions required. These measures were combined with
observations of navigation errors, hesitation and recovery behaviour to
identify areas requiring further refinement.

### Validation Summary

| Validation Measure | Target | Result |
|---|---:|---:|
| Task completion | ≥80% | **92.7%** |
| Acceptance criteria satisfied | ≥80% | **100%** |
| Confidence | ≥4/5 | **4.46/5** |
| Completed without assistance | ≥80% | **90%** |
| Error recovery | ≥80% | **90%** |

### Testing Outcome

The results indicate that the prototype successfully met the defined
usability targets, with an overall task completion rate of 92.7% and
average confidence of 4.46/5. All defined acceptance criteria were
successfully addressed, while 90% of tasks were completed without
facilitator assistance.

The results also identified specific areas for improvement. Create Chats,
Private Inbox, Rules & Policies and Building Documents produced the lowest
confidence scores and/or higher interaction counts. These findings were
used to refine navigation, improve the visibility of important actions,
clarify labels and reduce unnecessary interaction steps.
